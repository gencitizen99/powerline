What this tracks: (1) formal action by county governments on the proposed Valley North 765 kV line, and (2) every West Virginia state legislator who represents an impacted county, with their position as it becomes known. County resolutions and legislator statements aren’t binding on the PSC, but they shape the public record and the politics. Maintained by a community member — send corrections or updates with a link to the article, minutes, or statement.

Legislators are matched to counties from the official 2021 redistricting boundaries; a district counts if it covers any part of an impacted county. Developer contributions are from public campaign-finance filings (see Developer donations for sources). Positions reflect public statements known to the maintainer as of the date noted — blank means not yet known, not neutral. Nov 3, 2026 status comes from the official West Virginia Secretary of State returns for the May 2026 primary, plus minor-party and independent candidates registered with the state campaign-finance system (they do not appear in primary results, so that list may be incomplete — minor-party ballot access is certified separately by the Secretary of State). All 100 House seats are on the 2026 ballot; state Senate seats are staggered, so roughly half of senators are not up until 2028. Community information, not legal advice.
